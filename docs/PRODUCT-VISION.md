# LeMoSp — Product Vision: "Start Simple, Grow With Your Business"

> Owner: Leo Mboyerwa, LeMo Tech Solutions · Recorded 4 October 2026.
> This is the product's core functional philosophy. Every design or change to LeMoSp must follow it.
> The full original brief is kept below in the owner's own words; the summary comes first.

## Summary of the rules

1. **One platform, three progressive operating modes** — Small, Medium, Large/Enterprise. Never three separate apps.
   Modular architecture, feature flags, permissions, progressive disclosure, configurable rules.
2. **Level ≠ feature activation.** The level sets the *recommended default experience*. Individual features can be
   switched on for any company when it needs them (e.g. a small importer needs multi-currency).
3. **Owner declares a level at registration**, guided by a short questionnaire (employees, customers, suppliers,
   SKUs, warehouses, monthly sales, transactions, branches, imports, tenders, corporate clients, services, credit,
   approvals, business activities). The app recommends a level and explains it is *not a judgement* of the company.
   The owner can change it at any time.
4. **Upward growth never loses data** and never requires a new account.
5. **Growth engine**: the system measures real activity and recommends features/levels with *contextual reasons*
   ("Your catalogue has grown to 312 products…"), never generic "Upgrade now". Owner can view a tutorial, accept,
   postpone or dismiss. Thresholds and rules are configurable in the Admin Platform without code changes.
6. **Growth & Recommendations area**: current level, next level, reasons, features, benefits, guided demo.
7. **Demo Mode for all three levels** (Small / Medium / Enterprise), realistic fictional data, interactive guided
   tours through the real interface (what each module does, why, who uses it).
8. **Progressive feature discovery**: advanced features visible but marked "Coming as your business grows",
   "Recommended next step", "Available in Medium Mode".
9. **Alert engine** for operations *and* growth opportunities; configurable from Admin; in-app, dashboard, optional
   email, optional WhatsApp/SMS; users choose what they receive.
10. **Suggestion Box & continuous improvement**: categories (Sales, Customers, Procurement, Suppliers, Inventory,
    Warehouse, Finance, Accounting, Logistics, Employees, Technology, Reporting, Security, HSE, Business Growth,
    Other); routed to owner/managers; approve / reject / request clarification / assign / under review /
    implemented / archived; employees see their status; managers can create internal improvements and assign them.
11. **Admin Platform** (LeMo Tech): companies, users, plans, classifications, feature availability and activation,
    system configuration, recommendation thresholds, tutorials, demo data, alerts, announcements, suggestions and
    feedback, support, usage analytics, feature adoption, performance. Aggregated, anonymised feedback analytics feed
    a product-improvement engine.
12. **Adaptive navigation** by level, role, permissions and active features — never hundreds of modules at once;
    one common visual language across levels.
    - Small: Sales, Customers, Products, Inventory, Purchases, Expenses, Payments, Reports, Suggestions, Settings.
    - Medium adds: Procurement, CRM, Warehouses, Credit Management, Approvals, Analytics, Documents.
    - Enterprise adds: advanced procurement, logistics, finance, contracts, tenders, branches, audit trails,
      integrations, administration.
13. **Tanzania first**: TZS primary + USD/EUR/GBP/…; TIN/VRN, registration, VAT; local payment methods (bank,
    mobile money); regions/districts; tax and accounting configurable, not hard-coded. Scalable to East Africa.
14. **Multiple business models** per company (general supplier, distributor, wholesaler, importer, retailer,
    manufacturer-supplier, contractor, service provider). Rich product catalogue: types, UoM, brands, categories,
    specifications, part numbers, serials, batches, expiry, technical/safety documents, certificates, photos.
15. **Progressive business intelligence**: Small answers simple questions; Medium adds customer/product/supplier
    analysis, slow movers, cash expected, quotation conversion; Enterprise adds branch profitability, CLV, procurement
    savings, turnover, forecasting, budget vs actual. Later an AI business assistant over the company's own data,
    under strict permissions, always stating the period and data used.
16. **Mobile-first**, simple enough for a small Tanzanian owner on an Android phone, sophisticated enough for an
    enterprise procurement manager.

## The owner's original brief (verbatim)

Based on a modern Supplier Management, Business Operations, Procurement, Inventory, Sales, Customer Relationship Management, and Business Intelligence application designed primarily for supplier and general supply companies operating in Tanzania, with the long-term objective of making the platform scalable across East Africa and eventually other markets. The system should be designed around the principle that supplier companies do not all have the same level of size, complexity, capital, staffing, operational maturity, technology adoption, or business requirements. Therefore, I want the application to support three progressive business levels: Small-Scale Supplier, Medium-Scale Supplier, and Large-Scale/Enterprise Supplier. During registration and initial setup, the owner or administrator should be asked to select the approximate category that best represents the current size and operational maturity of the company. However, this classification must not permanently restrict the company. The application must continuously monitor business growth and operational activity and intelligently recommend when the company should move to the next level. The three levels should primarily be used to simplify the user experience, avoid overwhelming new or small businesses with unnecessary functionality, provide affordable and appropriate functionality at each stage, and gradually introduce more advanced capabilities as the company grows. The system should therefore be designed as a progressive business-management platform rather than three completely separate applications. A small supplier should be able to start with a simple and easy-to-use version of the system and, as the business grows, progressively unlock additional functionality until the company can eventually operate almost every aspect of its business through the full enterprise version.

For the purposes of this application, the Small-Scale Supplier should represent a micro or small general-supply business, individual entrepreneur, family-owned business, newly established company, or small trading company that may operate with a relatively small number of customers, suppliers, employees, products, orders, and transactions. These companies may supply items such as office supplies, cleaning materials, PPE, stationery, basic tools, electrical materials, plumbing materials, spare parts, lubricants, chemicals, food products, construction materials, agricultural supplies, or other general goods. They may operate from a small office, shop, warehouse, home office, or shared premises and may initially rely heavily on WhatsApp, phone calls, Excel, notebooks, paper quotations, mobile money, bank transfers, and manually prepared invoices. Their primary requirements are simplicity, visibility of sales, quotations, customers, suppliers, products, inventory, expenses, payments, and outstanding receivables. The Small-Scale version of the application should therefore focus on essential business management without introducing unnecessary complexity. Core features should include company profile and registration, user accounts, basic dashboard, customer management, supplier management, product and service catalogue, basic inventory tracking, quotation creation, sales orders, delivery records, invoices, receipts, expense recording, payment tracking, accounts receivable, basic purchase records, simple supplier balances, basic profit and loss visibility, document storage, basic notifications, simple activity history, and basic reports. The system should also provide simple stock alerts, low-stock alerts, overdue-payment alerts, quotation follow-up reminders, invoice reminders, pending-order notifications, and important business reminders. Small businesses should be able to record whether a customer has requested a quotation, whether the quotation was accepted or rejected, whether goods have been supplied, whether an invoice has been issued, whether payment has been received, and whether there is still an outstanding balance. The application should make these processes extremely simple and mobile-friendly because many small Tanzanian businesses may manage their operations primarily from Android smartphones. The Small-Scale version should also allow the owner to invite a limited number of employees and assign simple permissions such as sales, storekeeper, accountant, or administrator.

The Medium-Scale Supplier should represent an established and growing supplier company, typically operating with a larger customer base, more employees, more products, multiple suppliers, larger order volumes, larger inventories, more frequent purchases, and greater financial and operational complexity. The business may supply mining companies, construction companies, manufacturing companies, hotels, hospitals, schools, government institutions, NGOs, farms, food-processing companies, breweries, cement companies, energy companies, engineering companies, or other corporate clients. These companies may have multiple departments and may require formal procurement procedures, purchase orders, supplier quotations, delivery notes, goods received notes, invoice matching, stock management, customer credit management, approval workflows, and detailed reporting. The Medium-Scale version should therefore unlock advanced inventory management, multiple warehouses or storage locations, stock transfers, stock adjustments, batch and serial-number tracking where appropriate, minimum and maximum stock levels, reorder points, purchase requisitions, supplier quotations, purchase orders, goods received notes, supplier invoices, three-way matching where applicable, customer purchase orders, delivery notes, partial deliveries, backorders, credit limits, payment terms, customer statements, supplier statements, employee roles and permissions, approval workflows, expense categories, cash-flow tracking, bank and mobile-money reconciliation, more detailed financial reporting, sales-performance reports, purchasing reports, inventory valuation, gross-margin analysis, product profitability, customer profitability, supplier performance analysis, quotation conversion rates, sales pipeline tracking, recurring reminders, and more advanced notifications. The Medium-Scale system should also introduce CRM capabilities, allowing the company to track leads, opportunities, customer contacts, customer communication history, follow-ups, tenders, contracts, recurring customers, and important customer dates. It should support document attachments for tenders, contracts, certificates, tax documents, delivery documents, invoices, purchase orders, and other business records. It should also begin providing business intelligence and recommendations, such as identifying slow-moving inventory, frequently requested products, customers whose purchasing volume is increasing, customers with overdue payments, suppliers with poor delivery performance, products with declining margins, products that should be reordered, and opportunities for cross-selling or upselling.

The Large-Scale or Enterprise Supplier should represent a highly established, professionally managed, multi-departmental, multi-warehouse, multi-location, or high-volume supplier company. These businesses may serve large mining companies, multinational corporations, government institutions, construction groups, manufacturing companies, energy companies, hospitals, hospitality groups, large agricultural operations, international organizations, or major industrial customers. They may manage thousands or tens of thousands of products, multiple warehouses, multiple branches, large procurement operations, numerous employees, sales teams, logistics teams, finance departments, procurement departments, store departments, and management teams. Some may operate as distributors, importers, wholesalers, contractors, or integrated supply-chain businesses. The Enterprise version should provide the complete functionality of the platform and should support advanced inventory and warehouse management, multiple branches, multiple warehouses, multiple currencies, advanced purchasing, procurement workflows, tender management, contract management, supplier evaluation, customer credit management, advanced CRM, fleet and delivery management, logistics tracking, advanced financial reporting, profitability analysis, budgeting, cash-flow forecasting, purchase planning, demand forecasting, inventory forecasting, advanced approval workflows, departmental permissions, audit trails, role-based access control, multi-level approvals, advanced dashboards, management KPIs, branch performance comparison, salesperson performance, warehouse performance, supplier performance, customer profitability, product profitability, procurement analytics, business forecasting, automated reminders, scheduled reports, API integrations, accounting-system integrations, payment integrations, document management, advanced search, data export, advanced analytics, and enterprise-level security. The enterprise version should also be designed so that a company can operate several branches or business units under one organization while maintaining consolidated management reporting at head-office level.

The application should not rely solely on the owner's initial declaration of company size. During registration, the owner should select Small, Medium, or Large, but the application should also collect additional information such as approximate number of employees, number of active customers, number of suppliers, number of products or SKUs, number of warehouses, average monthly sales, approximate monthly transaction volume, number of branches, whether the company imports goods, whether the company participates in tenders, whether it supplies corporate clients, whether it provides services in addition to goods, whether it operates on credit, and whether it requires multiple approval levels. Based on this information, the application should recommend an initial configuration. The owner should be able to change the classification manually if necessary. The system must clearly explain that the classification is not a judgment of the quality of the company; it simply determines the complexity of the initial workspace and the features recommended for the company. The application should also allow a company to start as Small and gradually transition to Medium and eventually Enterprise without losing historical data, customers, suppliers, products, invoices, quotations, purchases, inventory records, or reports. The upgrade should be seamless and should never require the company to create a new account.

I also want a Demo Mode for all three levels. During registration, the user should be able to select "Explore Demo" before committing to a business configuration. The application should provide three separate interactive demo environments: Small Supplier Demo, Medium Supplier Demo, and Large/Enterprise Supplier Demo. Each demo should contain realistic but fictional sample data showing how the application works at that level. The Small Supplier Demo should demonstrate simple customer management, products, quotations, invoices, sales, purchases, stock, expenses, payments, and basic reports. The Medium Supplier Demo should demonstrate multiple customers and suppliers, inventory management, purchase orders, goods received notes, customer orders, credit management, CRM, approvals, warehouse operations, profitability, and management reports. The Large/Enterprise Demo should demonstrate multiple warehouses or branches, advanced procurement, tender management, contracts, approvals, logistics, advanced inventory, dashboards, financial analytics, business intelligence, forecasting, role-based access, audit trails, and management-level reporting. Each demo should include an interactive guided tour. Instead of merely displaying static screenshots, the tutorial should guide the user through the actual interface and explain what each important module does, why it is useful, who normally uses it, and how it improves business management. For example, when demonstrating inventory, the tutorial should explain stock-in, stock-out, transfers, adjustments, reorder levels, stock valuation, slow-moving stock, and inventory reports. When demonstrating procurement, it should explain supplier selection, quotation comparison, purchase orders, goods received notes, supplier invoices, and payment processes.

The system should implement a progressive tutorial and feature-discovery mechanism. When a Small Supplier enters the application, the system should initially display only the most important features while keeping advanced features available but clearly marked as "Coming as your business grows", "Recommended next step", or "Available in Medium Mode". As the business grows, the system should automatically detect indicators that suggest the company may benefit from additional functionality. For example, if the company increases the number of customers, employees, products, transactions, warehouses, purchase orders, invoices, outstanding receivables, or monthly revenue beyond configurable thresholds, the system should generate an intelligent recommendation. Instead of simply saying "Upgrade now", the alert should explain why the feature has become relevant. For example, if a small supplier begins managing hundreds of products, the system could display: "Your product catalogue and stock activity have grown significantly. Medium-level Inventory Management can help you track reorder levels, stock movement, supplier purchases, and slow-moving items. Would you like to see a short tutorial?" If a company begins supplying customers on credit, the system could recommend Customer Credit Management. If the company begins using multiple storage locations, it could recommend Multi-Warehouse Management. If employees increase, it could recommend Role-Based Access and Approval Workflows. If quotation activity increases, it could recommend CRM and Sales Pipeline Management. If the company begins participating in tenders, the system could recommend Tender and Contract Management.

The same progressive system should operate between Medium and Large/Enterprise. Medium-scale companies should receive recommendations when their operational complexity indicates that enterprise functionality would provide value. For example, if the company has several warehouses, multiple branches, hundreds or thousands of products, large procurement volumes, multiple departments, many employees, significant monthly sales, large numbers of customer accounts, frequent tenders, large outstanding receivables, high inventory value, or complex approval requirements, the system should recommend relevant Enterprise features. These recommendations should be intelligent and contextual rather than generic advertisements. The objective is to help the business owner understand that the system is growing together with their business.

There should be a dedicated "Growth & Recommendations" area in the application where the owner can see the current business level, the next recommended level, the reasons for the recommendation, the features that would become available, the potential business benefits, and a guided demonstration. The dashboard could display something such as "Your business is currently operating at Medium Level" and "Based on your recent activity, you may benefit from 4 Enterprise features." The system should show the features individually and allow the owner to watch a short tutorial before activating them. The owner should remain in control and should be able to postpone or dismiss recommendations. The system should not aggressively force upgrades. Instead, recommendations should be helpful, professional, and based on actual business activity.

The application should also contain an intelligent alert and notification system. Alerts should be generated both for operational issues and for business-development opportunities. Operational alerts may include low stock, stockouts, overdue invoices, approaching payment deadlines, delayed supplier deliveries, expired documents, expiring contracts, pending approvals, pending quotations, delayed customer orders, inventory discrepancies, abnormal stock movements, and other configurable events. Business-growth alerts should identify when the company may need a new feature, workflow, user role, warehouse, approval system, CRM capability, reporting capability, or higher business level. The alert engine should be configurable from the Admin Platform so that administrators can change thresholds and recommendation rules without modifying the application code. The system should support in-app notifications, dashboard alerts, optional email notifications, and optional WhatsApp/SMS integrations where technically and legally appropriate. Users should be able to configure which alerts they receive.

I also want a comprehensive Suggestion Box and Business Feedback System. Every company should have a Suggestion Box accessible from the application. Farmers are not relevant to this system; instead, the users should be business owners, managers, salespeople, procurement officers, storekeepers, accountants, logistics personnel, and other employees of supplier companies. Employees should be able to submit suggestions about how the company can improve its operations, reduce costs, improve customer service, improve inventory management, improve procurement, improve supplier relationships, improve sales, improve safety, improve productivity, or use the application more effectively. Suggestions should be classified into categories such as Sales, Customers, Procurement, Suppliers, Inventory, Warehouse, Finance, Accounting, Logistics, Employees, Technology, Reporting, Security, HSE, Business Growth, and Other. The suggestion should be routed to the appropriate company owner or authorized manager for review. The owner or manager should be able to approve, reject, request clarification, assign the suggestion to an employee, mark it as under review, mark it as implemented, or archive it. Employees should be able to see the status of their suggestions according to their permissions. The system should also allow the business owner or manager to create internal improvement suggestions and assign them to employees or departments. This turns the application into not only a business-recording system but also a continuous-improvement platform.

The central platform administrator should also have access to an aggregated, anonymized or permission-controlled analytics system showing the types of suggestions, feature requests, complaints, difficulties, and improvement ideas submitted across companies using the platform. This information should be used as a major source of product-development intelligence. For example, if many small suppliers request better quotation management, the product team should receive a recommendation to improve the quotation module. If many medium suppliers request multi-warehouse functionality, this should influence future development priorities. If users repeatedly struggle with a particular screen or process, the system should identify that pattern and recommend UI/UX improvements or additional tutorials. The system should therefore use user suggestions, feature usage, support issues, user behavior, and business-growth patterns as inputs into a product-improvement engine.

The Admin Platform should be significantly more powerful than the normal customer application. It should allow the platform owner to manage registered companies, user accounts, subscription plans if applicable, company classifications, feature availability, feature activation, system-wide configuration, recommendation thresholds, tutorials, demo data, alerts, notifications, suggestions, feedback, support requests, system announcements, usage analytics, feature adoption, and application performance. The Admin Platform should allow administrators to create or modify feature rules without hard-coding every recommendation. For example, an administrator should be able to define that when a company exceeds a configurable number of products, the system recommends advanced inventory management; when the company reaches a certain number of employees, role-based permissions are recommended; when the company operates more than one warehouse, multi-warehouse functionality is recommended; when monthly transaction volume reaches a certain threshold, advanced reporting is recommended; and when a company has multiple branches, enterprise branch management is recommended. These thresholds should be configurable because businesses differ across industries and countries.

The system should distinguish between "business level" and "feature activation." A company may technically remain classified as Small or Medium while being allowed to activate selected advanced features where necessary. For example, a small company may only have 50 employees but require multi-currency because it imports equipment. Therefore, the system should not unnecessarily block functionality solely because of the company's classification. The classification should primarily determine the recommended default experience, while individual features can be activated based on actual business needs, subscription rules, or administrator approval. This will make the system much more flexible and commercially viable.

The user interface should be modern, professional, clean, responsive, mobile-first, and easy enough for a small Tanzanian business owner with limited technical experience while still being sophisticated enough for a professional procurement manager or enterprise executive. The application should avoid presenting hundreds of modules on the screen at once. Navigation should adapt according to the user's business level, role, permissions, and activated features. A small supplier should see a simple dashboard with Sales, Customers, Products, Inventory, Purchases, Expenses, Payments, Reports, Suggestions, and Settings. A medium supplier should see additional modules such as Procurement, CRM, Warehouses, Credit Management, Approvals, Analytics, and Documents. An enterprise user should have access to a comprehensive navigation structure with advanced procurement, inventory, logistics, finance, CRM, contracts, tenders, branches, warehouses, analytics, approvals, audit trails, integrations, and administration. The design should nevertheless maintain a common visual language so that users moving from Small to Medium to Enterprise feel that they are using the same application rather than learning an entirely new system.

The system should also be designed specifically with the Tanzanian business environment in mind. It should support Tanzanian Shillings as the primary currency while allowing additional currencies such as USD, EUR, GBP, and others for companies that import or export. It should support Tanzanian company information, business registration information, tax identification information, VAT-related information where applicable, customer and supplier records, local payment methods, bank payments, mobile-money transactions where integrations are available, local addresses, regions, districts, and other relevant Tanzanian business information. The system should be designed so that tax and accounting functionality can be configured according to applicable Tanzanian requirements rather than hard-coded in a way that becomes difficult to update. It should also support companies that sell locally, import goods, distribute goods, provide services, or combine supply and contracting activities.

The application should support different supplier-business models. A company may be a general supplier, distributor, wholesaler, importer, retailer, manufacturer-supplier, contractor, service provider, or a combination of these. During setup, the company should select its primary business activities, but the architecture must allow multiple business activities under the same company. For example, a chemical and industrial supply company could sell chemicals, PPE, mechanical spares, lubricants, laboratory equipment, electrical equipment, and engineering services. The product catalogue should therefore support different product types, units of measure, brands, categories, specifications, part numbers, manufacturer part numbers, serial numbers, batch numbers, expiry dates where applicable, technical documents, safety documents, certificates, photographs, and supplier information.

The application should also progressively introduce advanced business intelligence. At the Small level, the system should answer simple questions such as "How much did I sell this month?", "Who owes me money?", "What products are low in stock?", and "What are my expenses?" At the Medium level, it should answer questions such as "Which customers generate the highest revenue?", "Which products generate the highest margin?", "Which suppliers deliver late?", "What inventory is not moving?", "How much cash should I expect next month?", and "Which quotations are most likely to convert?" At the Enterprise level, it should provide advanced management intelligence such as branch profitability, customer lifetime value, supplier performance, procurement savings, inventory turnover, demand forecasting, working-capital requirements, sales forecasting, gross-margin trends, budget versus actual performance, departmental performance, and business-growth forecasting. The application should eventually include an AI-powered business assistant capable of answering questions about the company's own data, subject to strict permissions and security. For example, an authorized owner could ask, "Which five customers increased their purchases the most during the last six months?", "Which products have been out of stock most frequently?", or "What are my largest outstanding receivables?" The AI should provide answers based on the company's actual data and clearly indicate the period and data used.

The onboarding experience should be particularly important. After registration, the application should ask the owner a short series of questions about the business and then recommend the appropriate starting configuration. The system should explain the three business levels in simple language before the owner selects one. It should provide a comparison such as Small = essential tools for starting and organizing the business; Medium = advanced tools for growing sales, inventory, procurement, customers, and employees; Large/Enterprise = complete tools for complex operations, multiple locations, departments, and large-scale procurement and distribution. The onboarding process should also offer "Watch Small Demo", "Watch Medium Demo", and "Watch Enterprise Demo" options. The user should be able to explore the demos before deciding. After selecting a level, the system should provide a short personalized tutorial showing only the most relevant modules first.

The entire architecture should be designed so that the system can evolve with the company. A business that starts with five customers, twenty products, one employee, and manual stock records should be able to begin using the application immediately without feeling that the system is too complicated. Five years later, the same company might have 2,000 customers, 10,000 products, five warehouses, 50 employees, several branches, multiple currencies, large procurement contracts, tenders, and corporate clients. The system should allow that company to grow into the Enterprise environment without migrating to another software platform. Historical records, documents, relationships, reports, and transaction data should remain intact throughout the entire journey.

The ultimate philosophy of the product should be "Start Simple, Grow With Your Business." The application should not merely categorize companies into Small, Medium, and Large; it should actively help companies move from one stage to the next by identifying operational problems, recommending better processes, introducing new features at the right time, providing tutorials, measuring business performance, collecting user suggestions, and giving management actionable information. The system should function as a digital business partner rather than simply an electronic record-keeping tool. Every feature should have a clear business purpose, and the system should continuously ask, through data and user feedback, how it can help the supplier reduce costs, increase sales, improve customer service, reduce stock losses, improve procurement, manage cash flow, improve employee accountability, strengthen supplier relationships, and ultimately grow the business.

When designing or modifying the application, treat the above requirements as the product's core functional philosophy. Do not create three completely independent applications. Build one scalable platform with a modular architecture, feature flags, permissions, progressive disclosure, configurable business-level rules, intelligent recommendations, guided tutorials, demo environments, and an administration platform. The Small, Medium, and Enterprise classifications should be treated as progressive operating modes within the same platform. The system should always preserve the user's data and allow upward progression. The Admin Platform should have complete visibility and control over feature configuration, recommendations, tutorials, suggestions, user feedback, system analytics, and platform improvement. The final result should be a professional, scalable, secure, mobile-friendly Supplier Management and Business Operating System that is particularly useful to Tanzanian supplier companies and can later be expanded to other African markets and industries.
